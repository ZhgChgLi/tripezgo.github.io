Why it exists

The hard part isn’t choosing where to go. It’s the order.

Build it in a spreadsheet and you do the arithmetic yourself, look up every connection yourself, paste in every ticket yourself. Then three things happen.

01

It scatters

The plan in a notes app, the flight in email, the hotel confirmation in a chat thread, the restaurant in a screenshot. On the ground, finding the ticket takes longer than getting to the place.

02

The timing is guesswork

How long that walk takes, when the next train leaves — you look each one up by hand. Miss a single leg and the rest of the day goes with it.

03

Plans always change

A closure, a stop that ran long, traffic. Changing it is such a chore that most people don’t — and from day two the plan in your hand isn’t true any more.

Features

Everything a trip needs, in one app

No switching between a calendar, a map, a notes app and your camera roll.

One timeline, a whole day that fits

Calendar and timeline views. Long-press to add, drag to change the time — there is no “tap Edit first”. All-day things like a rental car or a typhoon warning pin to their own header. Push an event earlier or later and everything after it moves with it; cancel one and it stays in place, struck through, instead of vanishing.

The stretch between two stops, worked out for you

Walking and driving times are estimated from the map. For public transport you can pick a mode and save several departures — the one you chose is shown first, and you can switch to a backup any time. When the estimated travel time is longer than the gap you left, it tells you before you find out the hard way.

Will the day work? See it on the map first

Every located stop that day is connected in order, and that line is the leg — tap it to view or set it. Drop your own pins on places you want to go, or share them in from another app. A rich built-in map of convenience stores and other shops is included, and you can get a walking route from wherever you are.

Everything filed under the thing it belongs to

The ticket goes on the train, the reservation on the restaurant, the reminder on “before you leave”. Links, photos and PDFs attach to the event itself and open with one tap; saved links keep their cookies, so you don’t get logged out. Documents you need for the whole trip — a rail pass, insurance — live in the overview instead of six days deep.

One trip, planned together

Share a trip with the people coming along — one invite link is all it takes. Everyone edits the same itinerary, notes and shopping list, and you can see who changed what. Turn sharing off whenever you like and the trip is yours alone again.

The tools you use on the road, on one page

One page, three tabs: the shopping list, currency conversion and notes. The shopping list remembers where you saw it and which shop was cheapest — snap a photo, or share one in from another app, and it’s saved. The converter works offline. Notes belong to a trip, so rental-car rules and whatever the host told you stay under that trip; a Japanese travel phrasebook comes with them.

Widgets and Live Activities, built into the Apple ecosystem

Once a trip starts, a Live Activity shows the current event, the time left and the next stop on the Lock Screen — no unlocking. Widgets put today’s events, files and to-dos on your Home Screen. Apple Calendar and Siri Shortcuts are supported too, and you can share links, photos and places straight in from other apps.

Works offline, private by default

No account, no server of ours

View and edit your trips with no connection. Your data is stored on this device and synced between your own devices through your own iCloud account — never kept on a third-party server.

  • No sign-up. Install it and plan day one — no account, no password.
  • No tracking, no analytics SDKs. There is no third-party analytics or ad code in the app.
  • Sync runs on your iCloud. Data goes into your Apple ID’s private database; only your devices can open it.
  • Sharing runs on iCloud too. Sharing a trip uses Apple’s own iCloud sharing; the invite link is simply the key, and it never passes through a server of ours.
  • Backup and export are free. Back up a whole library to one file, or export a trip as .ics.
